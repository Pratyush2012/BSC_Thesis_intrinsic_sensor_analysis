\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}

The thesis set out to test whether terrain type can be reliably classified from accelerometer, gyroscope and wheel-odometry data alone, on a small wheeled agricultural robot and without any external perception sensor. The answer is affirmative at the operating point the pipeline was designed for. On the primary farm dataset the best classifier reaches macro-$F_1 \approx 0.87$ under Leave-One-Run-Out cross-validation, with all six classifier families clustered within one cross-fold standard deviation. The same pipeline reaches $F_1 \approx 0.98$ on a matched three-class campus subset, locating the residual farm difficulty in the intrinsic similarity of the \texttt{grass}--\texttt{soil} pair rather than in the pipeline itself. The accelerometer and gyroscope dominate the information budget, either inertial group can carry the terrain signature on its own, while odometry is redundant at inference. A CUSUM transition detector on the posterior stream operates at $3$--$6\,\text{s}$ mean delay against a $\sim 1$/min false-alarm budget, comfortably within an intercropping manoeuvre's latency tolerance. Together these results support intrinsic terrain classification as a lightweight, always-available complement to exteroceptive sensing on a small agricultural field robot.

\section{Future Work}

Four directions follow directly from the limitations identified in \Cref{sec:disc_limitations}:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Widen the in-field discrimination.} The \texttt{grass}--\texttt{soil} margin is the operationally binding number (\Cref{sec:disc_deployment}). Per-wheel tyre-slip, motor-current variance, or a small microphone, all reported to help in the broader proprioceptive literature~\cite{DuPont2008,Bermudez2012}, are the most direct levers for lifting the $F_1 \approx 0.76$ on \texttt{grass}.
    \item \textbf{Expand the data envelope.} A second farm site, and additional sessions covering different vegetation stages, soil moisture and weather windows, would turn the current cross-day evaluation into a true cross-condition one.
    \item \textbf{Extend the transition detector.} CUSUM was evaluated only on the farm; extending it to the multi-speed campus dataset and comparing against Bayesian online change-point detection~\cite{Adams2007} would test the calibration argument under harder conditions.
    \item \textbf{Move the pipeline online.} All inference here was offline. A real-time ROS\,2 deployment on Ricbot, exposing the per-window posterior to the navigation stack, is the natural next milestone for the intercropping project.
\end{itemize}
