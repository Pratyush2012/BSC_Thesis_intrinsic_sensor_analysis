\section{Terrain Classification for Mobile Robots}

Terrain recognition has been an active research topic in mobile robotics since the
early 2000s.
Early work by Brooks and Iagnemma \cite{Brooks2005} demonstrated that the vibration
spectrum recorded by an IMU mounted on a wheeled robot correlates strongly with
surface properties such as roughness, compaction, and surface texture.
By extracting spectral features from short time windows and feeding them to a Gaussian
mixture model, they achieved reliable discrimination between grass, gravel, and
concrete in outdoor settings.

Subsequent work extended this paradigm in several directions.
Vibration signals from wheel--surface interaction were used for proprioceptive terrain
estimation on planetary rovers \cite{Weiss2011}, where cameras cannot always be
relied on due to lighting conditions or dust.
Several authors explored learning-based approaches — k-nearest neighbours, support
vector machines, and random forests — as alternatives to hand-crafted GMM classifiers,
noting that richer feature sets generally outperformed simpler spectral summaries
\cite{Ojeda2006}.
